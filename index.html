On-site assessment

Lives much more like a 2-bedroom / 1-bath

From our walkthrough, there is not a true additional basement bedroom. The unfinished laundry area contains a toilet and sink rather than what most buyers would experience as a conventional second bathroom.

The basement may still add usable flexibility, but its utility should not be valued as though it offers a genuine extra bedroom and full living suite.

Why that matters: Comparing this home at face value with finished, fully functional 3-bedroom / 2-bath homes risks overpaying. We should value actual utility, quality and layout alongside location and documented square footage—not give every reported square foot or room identical weight.

Our approach after going under contract

We would hope to negotiate additional consideration if the inspections uncover legitimate issues. That could mean repairs, a seller credit or a price adjustment, depending on what is found and the contract. We cannot count on a second price reduction. Any inspection request should be supported by actual findings and addressed within the contract deadlines.

Equally important: we must be prepared to walk away. A beautiful home in a favorite neighborhood can quickly feel irreplaceable. It is not. We want to buy a home we love at a price and risk level that make sense, rather than letting attachment dictate our decision.

Before an offer, verify the basement's actual room and bathroom functionality, permits and square footage, financing/appraisal implications, and any major systems that need further investigation. Do not waive important protections simply to secure the property.
